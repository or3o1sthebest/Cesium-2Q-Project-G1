Voices That Matter: Nurturing Mental Health in a Fast-Paced World

In a world that never stops moving, silence can sometimes feel deafening. The simple “what-ifs” often spiral into a storm of thoughts that pull you deeper into your own mind, making each day a battle against your emotions. The feeling of suffocation grows as life becomes more complicated than it should be, yet everything could change with a simple question: “How have you been?” Though it may seem ordinary, this small act of communication can make a powerful impact by encouraging people to open up, seek help, and better understand their emotions. It is an essential step in promoting mental health in todays fast-paced and technology-driven world.

Mental health refers to a person’s emotional, psychological, and social well-being, influencing how one thinks, feels, and behaves. It affects how individuals handle stress, build relationships, and make decisions in daily life. Mental health covers a wide range of conditions, from common concerns such as anxiety and depression to more complex disorders like bipolar disorder and schizophrenia. It is shaped by a mix of genetic, biological, environmental, and lifestyle factors, making it a vital part of overall health that deserves the same attention as physical well-being.

Dr. Mary Glenda A. Lugtu, a registered psychologist, highlighted the importance of resilience in facing challenges related to mental health during the Mental Health Seminar conducted for the scholars of the Philippine Science High School – SOCCSKSARGEN Region Campus (PSHS-SRC) in Koronadal City on October 24, 2025. According to Dr. Lugtu, addressing and resolving problems is essential in managing common mental health issues such as depression and anxiety. She explained that developing a mindset focused on finding and completing solutions builds strength and adaptability. “If there’s a problem, we should always fix it, so that each time you encounter one, your mindset is to find a solution. Not just to start fixing it, but to finish fixing it, and that is grit,” Lugtu said. Grit refers to the persistence and determination to keep moving forward despite difficulties, allowing individuals to overcome challenges through consistency and effort.

Expanding on this idea, it is equally important to recognize the hardships people face during difficult times. Acknowledging their struggles through small acts of empathy and understanding can ease the emotional weight they carry. When individuals feel seen and supported, they are more likely to open up and seek help. Promoting open conversations about mental health in schools, workplaces, and online spaces is key to breaking the stigma and building a society that values compassion and emotional well-being.

Building on these efforts to strengthen mental wellness, FPJ Panday Bayanihan Party-List Representative Brian Poe has filed House Resolution No. 428, calling for the nationwide enhancement of mental health support systems across schools, communities, and digital platforms. The measure was prompted by new data showing that seven in ten Filipinos have experienced bullying, many still struggling with its long-term psychological effects. Poe emphasized the urgency of improving access to mental health resources and implementing preventive measures, particularly for the youth who face increasing emotional strain from both in-person and cyberbullying. Through this resolution, he hopes to promote a more compassionate and proactive approach to mental health, ensuring that every Filipino receives the care and support needed to heal, grow, and thrive.

As we go through our daily lives, facing challenges and pushing through no matter how heavy they become, it is important to remember that we do not have to face them alone. Reach out to a friend, a family member, or even your guidance counselor, and let them know how you feel. Sometimes, the simple act of sharing your thoughts can lighten the weight you carry. After all, every journey becomes easier and every destination feels closer when you have someone walking beside you.
